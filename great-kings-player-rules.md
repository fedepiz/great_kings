# THE GREAT KINGS — The Rules of the Game
*Complete and exactly as implemented in this build. The MAP is the sole authority on adjacency, coasts, farm yields and resource sites.*

## 1. Board and powers
36 regions. Five powers, each a court seated at a permanent HOME (Mycenae, Hattusa, Washukanni, Babylon, Lower Egypt). A home is absolute: it cannot be entreated, subverted, treatied or peacefully taken. Every other region is a polity you hold at some relation. Wild peoples (Ister, Kaska, Lukka, Sutu, Apiru, Shasu, Libu, Sherden & Shekelesh) accept no construction and cap at Ally; the Lukka and the Sherden are seafarers. All regions currently behave "Soft": open, peaceable, buildable at Friend+.

## 2. Relations
Per (player, region): INFLUENCE (a number) and STATUS (a rung). Floors: Friend 2 · Ally 5 · Subject 10.
- Friend (2, non-exclusive): trade with the region and transit through it; entreat paths cross it; build on open ground there — never in wild country, which accepts no construction at any rung (§1). Establishes AUTOMATICALLY the moment influence reaches 2.
- Ally (5, exclusive): all Friend rights; the region answers your call in war with AT MOST ONE garrison, for 1 influence (§9).
- Subject (10, exclusive): their region is inside your writ — every building standing there answers your command, levy freely, build freely, seat governments.
- Climbing past Friend takes a TREATY (a palace command, target in reach): Friend→Ally needs influence ≥5 AND exceeding any incumbent Ally/Subject's influence there (the incumbent falls to Friend); Ally→Subject needs ≥10 and the same overtake rule. Wild peoples never submit.
- Decay at upkeep: a SEATED province (any seat building of yours present) loses 1 influence flat; everywhere else only influence above your floor fades by 1.
- Strain-then-fall: below your floor at upkeep marks the relation STRAINED; still below at the NEXT upkeep, it breaks one rung. Losing Subject status EVICTS your government: every seat building there is torn out at −2 influence each.
- Raiding a region zeroes your influence and status there.

## 3. Goods, stores, the Food Store
Four goods: food, bronze, cloth, pottery. Your STORES hold everything you own and everything you can spend; they fill by levy, by trade and by routed plunder, and nothing is turned away during the year — no cap binds you while the year runs.
Grain does not keep. Your FOOD STORE is what survives the turning of the year: 1 for your royal palace, and 2 for every granary standing in your home or your Subjects. Food beyond it spoils at the reckoning. Bronze, cloth and pottery keep without limit.

## 4. Time and turns
Play in YEARS; within a year the table goes around in ROUNDS until every power passes, then upkeep closes the year. On your turn you take ONE action: activate ONE untapped building at your command — the home palace, one of your seat buildings, or a market or port standing in any region where you hold Friend+. Taps clear only at year's end: each building acts once per YEAR. New buildings are constructed TAPPED (idle until next year). An activation not yet acted on may be cancelled (the tap reverts). If your home ever lacks a palace you may spend your turn restoring it (built tapped).

## 5. Actors and their commands
- ROYAL PALACE (home only, exactly one): 3 commands. All verbs.
- Seat buildings (1 command each, acting from their own region): CHANCERY — entreat or subvert, reach 2. STEWARD — levy or build, reach 1, ordinary buildings only. STABLES — one land raid, reach 3. WARRIORS — one raid, reach 2, and the only unit that sails. FORTRESS — never acts: while untapped it adds +1 to its own region's defense.
- MARKET / PORT: these belong to the region that holds them; trading is the building's own action, and any power at Friend+ may open it — but it taps, so the first to open it that year has it.
WHOSE IS IT: only the ROYAL PALACE and the five SEAT BUILDINGS belong to a crown. Every other work on the map — farms, workshops, markets, ports, granaries, garrisons — belongs to the region it stands in, whoever paid to raise it, and serves whichever power the province serves.
Seat buildings stand only in your home or Subjects, one of each kind per region, and may be raised in ANY Subject regardless of range (ordinary buildings keep build range 1). Placing your first seat building seats a government. Every seat building placed flatters the province +1 influence; every one removed or lost angers it −2. Each eats 1 food at upkeep.

## 6. Verbs (each costs one command)
- BUILD: target in range 1 of the actor, at Friend+ (or your home/Subject), with legal open ground (the building belongs to the region) — workshops need their matching resource slot (copper/cloth/clay), ports a sea slot, farms a region with farm yield ≥1. Cost: 1 food. Ordinary buildings (farm, market, port, granary, garrison) demolish freely via REMOVE (palace only, home/Subjects); removing a seat building costs the −2.
- LEVY: range 1 from the home palace or a steward. ONE region per command, and it must be your home or a Subject: EVERY untapped producer there is swept at once, proceeds to your stores.
- ENTREAT: target in diplomatic reach (never a home), at most ONCE per target per YEAR. +1 influence per DISTINCT good gifted, food included, maximum +4; empty hands earn nothing.
- TREATY: the climb, as in §2.
- SUBVERT: target in reach where your influence ≥2 and at least one other power has standing, at most ONCE per target per YEAR. You spend the command to open a BIDDING for the province's elite; every power with influence there takes part, each against all. One round, poorest standing bidding first (ties by turn order), each laying a BASKET of goods that stays with the province win or lose. A basket that matches-or-beats EVERY other in EVERY good, with at least one strict excess, wins; anything else is a tie and nothing moves. The winner loses nothing; every other participant loses influence equal to the WINNER'S standing in that province — so the blow lands as heavily as the winner is entrenched. A sovereign may subvert their own Subject to sweep rivals out of it.
- RAID / SEA RAID: §9.

## 7. Paying for things (sourcing)
Every cost is paid EITHER entirely from your stores, OR by tapping producers in ONE region — the actor's own or one adjacent, and only your home or Subjects (command production; never allied or friendly ground). Tap as many of that province's producers as the bill needs; distinct goods among the taps become entreat gifts (one each); what a producer's yield exceeds the bill stays with the province — you requisition the work, not the harvest. To take a harvest, levy it. A GRANARY may be tapped to issue 1 food from your stores into the local channel — the one bridge from purse to province. No mixing purse and taps in one action.

## 8. Trade
A MARKET buys overland at range 3, crossing Friend+ (or your own) ground. A PORT buys QUAY TO QUAY: its counterparties are regions that THEMSELVES hold an active port, at Friend+, and the source tapped must stand in that harbour region — cargo never walks inland. Either way: tap one untapped producer at the endpoint, in a region where you hold Friend+ (or your own) — and extract its FULL yield. Pay 1 good per good extracted, each of a DIFFERENT type than the extracted good; afford the whole yield or the deal is illegal.
- A trade may be PAID BY TAPS (one sponsoring region, §7) or PAID FROM STORES. Either way the proceeds come to your stores. What differs is what you spend: a tap costs a producer its year and reaches only your home and Subjects; the purse costs goods and reaches anywhere.
Payment goes to whoever holds the province: a region under a rival's writ — that rival gains it; a region under your own — it returns to your stores, and the trade is a swap; a region under no crown — the locals absorb it. Every trade with a source region below Subject warms it +1 influence (Friend establishes at 2); your own Subjects are not warmed by purchase.

## 9. War
A raid is LAND or SEA, never mixed. Land: garrisons (reach 1), warriors (2), stables (3) march through Friend+ (or your own) ground. Sea: ONLY warriors sail, each from a region with an untapped port (a berth each); the target needs only a coast.
- ASSEMBLY: click units to commit; prices live and refundable until launch. Your own units are free. An allied region (Ally+) answers a call with AT MOST ONE garrison, for 1 influence — and a called unit is tapped, its year spent. Wild PEOPLES adjacent to the battlefield — plus the seafaring peoples at any sea raid — and unclaimed by an Ally+ patron negotiate as peoples, not units: each side may lay a BASKET of goods before them. The basket that matches-or-beats the other in EVERY good, with at least one strict excess, wins their favour, and exactly ONE warband joins the winner (a tie or an unmet offer moves no one). Every good laid before a people stays with them, win or lose. An Ally+ patron closes the bidding entirely: the patron may call ONE of that people's warbands as an ordinary ally, and the people never fight against their patron. Seafarers answering a sea raid never fight FOR the defence — a defender's winning basket only buys them off and sends them home: danegeld.
- LAUNCH burns ALL your influence in the target (status to stranger) and costs THE FOREMOST — whoever stands highest in the province, ties included — 1 influence there.
- DEFENSE: every power at Friend+ in the target is a protector; they take the field in turn, lowest influence first, always by land. Each protector's muster is AUTOMATIC and MINIMAL: they tap exactly as many of their own reachable units as the deficit demands, never more — and every unit that musters is tapped, its year spent. What remains in a protector's hands during their muster turn is silver: counter-bid for the wild peoples, or pay the seafarers to leave. A target with no protector defends with its own militia, auto-committing exactly what is needed. An untapped fortress adds +1 to its own region.
- RESOLUTION: attack ≤ defense — repelled, committed forces spent. Attack > defense — (A−D) STRIKES, chosen one building at a time: an UNTAPPED building is overrun (tapped; a producer surrenders its full yield to the attacker); an already-TAPPED building is DESTROYED, and THE FOREMOST loses 2 influence there — the protection was theirs to give. The foremost is reckoned afresh for every work torn down, so a long raid walks down the ladder, striking whoever stands highest at that moment.

## 10. Upkeep (closes every year)
1. No harvest: nothing arrives unbidden — food on hand is what levy and trade brought.
2. Mouths: your palace and every seat building eat 1 food each (garrisons eat nothing), from your stores. Shortfall forces ABANDONMENT: you choose which eaters fall (a seat lost angers −2).
3. The reckoning: food beyond your Food Store spoils. Bronze, cloth and pottery keep.
4. Relations check: decay, then strain-then-fall (§2), with subject-loss evictions.
5. Everything untaps; the embassy ledger clears; a new year begins.

## 11. Forfeit
A player may dissolve at any time: palaces and seat buildings are pulled down; every other building was always the region's and simply remains; all ties zero; the home becomes ordinary courtable ground; the player takes no further turn. Forfeiting takes TWO words — the first arms it, the second carries it out — so no single misclick ends a court.

## 12. Not in this build
Dispositions beyond universal Soft, events, scoring and victory, campaigns, player-to-player trade, fisheries. The game currently ends when the players stop.

## 13. Advice to the table — tips & pitfalls
*(Skippable: nothing below is a rule, and no rule above depends on it.)*

GLOSSARY — the words that matter: COMMAND: one verb use; every verb costs exactly ONE command; "rN" always means RANGE N, never a cost. SEAT BUILDING: one of exactly five — chancery, stables, steward, fortress, warrior; no other building is ever a seat building. SEAT: a province where one of YOUR seat buildings stands. PRODUCER: a building that yields goods when tapped — farm, bronze works, dye works, potteries. EMBASSY: a single entreat bearing gifts; an action, not a building. TAPPED: has acted this year; taps clear only after upkeep at year's end. FLOOR: the influence a rank never falls below (Friend 2 / Ally 5 / Subject 10). STRAINED (!): below its floor; breaks at next upkeep unless restored. THE FOOD STORE: what survives the turning of the year — 1 for the palace, 2 per granary; food above it spoils.

GOALS, in order: 1) STAY FED — before your action ends, upkeep due must be covered by your stores — the panel shows the due beside them; levy your best food region most rounds. 2) HOLD YOUR SUBJECTS — a strained subject (marked !) breaks NEXT upkeep; entreat it this round. 3) CLIMB ONE LADDER AT A TIME — focus gifts on one target until a treaty lands; scattered single gifts evaporate against decay. 4) DIVERSIFY GOODS — bronze/cloth/pottery make richer embassies; build on resource sites, trade for what you lack. 5) USE GEOGRAPHY — court regions adjacent to your realm first; range is real.

TIPS & PITFALLS — the commonest failures at this table, each with a worked example. Read these AGAINST your plan before acting:

1. THE LONE-GIFT EMBASSY. Mycenae once sent 1 food to Wilusa, a stranger: +1 influence, landing at 1 — below the Friend floor of 2. A stranger below the floor decays to NOTHING at upkeep: the good and the command bought a memory. The same embassy with TWO distinct goods (food + pottery) lands at 2, Friend establishes automatically, and the floor holds it forever. A lone gift on a stranger achieves nothing BY ITSELF — but influence stacks within the year: any trade with that region warms it +1 (plain commodity trade counts), so a 1-good embassy plus one same-year trade with the same region = 2 = Friend, durable. Either bring 2 distinct goods, or bring 1 and close the gap by trading with them before upkeep.

2. LEVYING WHAT A LOCAL TAP WOULD PAY. Hatti once spent a palace command levying Tarhuntassa for food, then paid a build there from its stores — two commands to move grain in a circle. The build could have been SPONSORED at the site: tap Tarhuntassa's own farm as the payment, zero extra commands. Levy is for BANKING a surplus; local bills are paid by local taps.

3. RUNNING OUT OF FOOD. Egypt once gifted away its last 2 food in embassies and reached upkeep with mouths unfed — the palace and every seat building each eat 1. Shortfall forces ABANDONMENT: a seat torn down at −2 influence, often straining the province toward breaking. Count mouths FIRST: your stores must cover the upkeep due BEFORE any food leaves as gifts or payments.

4. ONE GOOD, ONE JOB. Mycenae once planned "entreat Alashiya with pottery, then trade that pottery for bronze" — one pottery, two jobs. When two steps of a plan claim the same unit of a good, the plan is broken NOW, not at step two: reassign or resequence before acting, and re-read your stock after every step that spends.

5. EXPAND WHEN THE ENGINE CARRIES IT, AND BUILD A GRANARY WHEN GRAIN ROTS. A seat is not paid for once: it eats 1 food EVERY year, for ever, and angers the province −2 if ever lost. Grain does not keep, so a fat year buys nothing towards the next one — what matters is not the surplus sitting in your stores but whether your levies and food-buying markets bring in the mouths' worth every single year. Two plain signals tell you which way you are facing. If food SPOILS at the reckoning, you are bringing in more than you can hold: build a granary — it saves the waste and widens what you may carry (2 more, on top of the palace's 1). If instead you scrape the due bare each year, you are already at your ceiling: raise no new seats until the food engine grows.

6. THE LARDER RAID. An undefended farm — no protector, at most militia — is a levy with spears: each strike on an untapped producer plunders its full yield. Worth it ONLY when the sums hold: launching burns ALL your influence in the target, and wild peoples nearby may be bid into the defence. Against truly bare ground it is often the cheapest food on the map.

7. THE RAID AS A CROWBAR. A raid is also influence warfare, and it falls on THE FOREMOST — whoever stands highest in the province at that moment. The launch tests them for −1; every already-TAPPED building your strikes destroy shames them a further −2. The foremost is reckoned afresh at every blow, so a long raid does not grind one rival into the ground: it walks down the ladder, striking whoever stands highest each time. Where one power towers over a province, the first blows all land on them; where two are close, the damage spreads across both. Against a rival's province held near a floor, a won raid can pry their grip loose faster than any embassy of yours could climb — so when choosing what your strikes hit, untapped buildings pay you in plunder, but tapped ones pay you in the rival's standing. Pick by what you need. This is how you crack UP into a rival's sphere; to crack DOWN and clear rivals out of your own, see tip 8.

8. THE BIDDING FOR THE ELITE — A WEAPON OF THE ENTRENCHED. Hatti held Kizzuwatna as Subject at 10, with Mitanni nosing in at 4 and Egypt at 3. Hatti opened a subversion in its OWN province, won it, and both rivals were swept to nothing. Cost: one command and a basket of goods. Had Hatti raided instead, the launch would have burned Hatti's own 10 to zero — you cannot drive rivals out of your sphere with spears without abandoning the sphere yourself. That is the shape of it, and it surprises: the blow is the WINNER'S standing, not the loser's. A court at 3 takes 3 from each other participant; a court at 10 takes 10. So subversion pays the strong, and it is the only instrument that CRACKS DOWN — the one way to clear foreign friends out of ground you already hold. To crack UP, raid instead (tip 7): against a province a rival dominates you have almost no standing to lose, so the launch costs you nothing that matters, and every blow falls on the foremost — which is them. Two cautions. Ties are the usual outcome once three or more bid, since winning means matching-or-beating EVERY other basket in EVERY good with at least one strict excess — and everything laid down stays with the province whether you win, lose or tie: an evenly contested subversion burns four courts' goods and moves nothing. And the poorest standing bids FIRST, so the weak bid blind while the strongest lays last, having seen every basket. The advantage compounds — the entrenched hit hardest, bid last, and lose nothing by winning.

9. USE THE WHOLE ACTIVATION. Mycenae once opened its palace (3 commands), sent one fine embassy to Alashiya, and ended the activation — journalling that Ugarit "must wait for a later palace command." There is no later palace command: the palace acts ONCE per year, and unspent commands die with the activation. Two commands were buried while Iolkos' grain stood unlevied. Before ending any activation, ask of every remaining command: what would this buy me today? Spend it — UNLESS spending would be genuinely wasteful: burning goods earmarked for a planned embassy or trade, levying producers you would rather leave for a sponsoring tap, or courting a target you cannot yet court well. Deliberate thrift is play; forgetting the budget is not.

10. TRADE BEFORE THE THRONE. The palace is not the only door into a year, and it is rarely the best first one. Markets, ports and raids are their own activations — no palace command spent — and their proceeds arrive BEFORE any later activation of the same year. A court that bee-lines for the palace with empty stores finds its embassies unaffordable; the same court that spends round 1 on a trade paid from stores (or a larder raid) opens the palace in round 2 with the gift-goods already in hand. When your palace plan needs goods you lack, the order is: earn first, court after — the year has many rounds, and the throne can wait one.

11. THE MAP'S GOODS BELONG TO REGIONS, NOT RULERS. Friend is a non-exclusive rung: you may entreat a province even while it sits at Subject under a rival crown, and Friend with the REGION opens its market to your traders regardless of who governs it. Mycenae lacking bronze may court Hatti's own Kizzuwatna to Friend — two distinct gifts, or one gift plus a same-year trade — and then buy from its producers by market or port like anyone else. Every such trade warms YOUR standing inside their sphere (+1), a quiet foothold under the rival's floor. One thing to know about the silver: the works belong to the province, never to whoever paid to raise them, so your payment goes to the crown whose writ the province lies under — or to the locals, if it lies under none.

12. THE TURNING OF THE YEAR. Grain that will not be eaten will rot: your Food Store is 1 for the palace and 2 per granary, and everything above it spoils at the reckoning. So food late in the year is nearly free to spend — send it as gifts, pay it into trades, lay it before the wild peoples. A granary is how you argue with the winter; an embassy is how you spend what you cannot keep.

Further pitfalls, compact: ending the year short of food (famine forces abandonments); leaving a strained (!) holding unrestored — it breaks next upkeep; raising seat buildings faster than your food grows (each eats 1/year); a stranger courted with a single gift (nothing sticks below Friend); raids whose spoils don't cover their costs.

THE COURTSHIP YEAR: one embassy per target per year is the design, not a nuisance — a year's courtship is one scene, so bring your best basket; you cannot return until next year and decay tolls the crossing. This is why goods diversity is power and why trade exists. But the embassy is not the only channel: trading with a region's producers warms it +1 per trade, and placing a seat building flatters it +1 — a full-court press uses all three. Upkeep (once per YEAR) eats 1 food per palace and per seat building; famine forces abandonments. There is no victory condition yet.
